---
name: client-education
description: Write client education handouts for common veterinary conditions
---

Create a comprehensive, client-friendly educational handout about the veterinary condition, procedure, or topic provided.

The handout should include:

**Title and Introduction**
- Clear, descriptive title (e.g., "Understanding Diabetes in Cats")
- Brief, empathetic introduction acknowledging that receiving this diagnosis can be concerning

**What Is It?**
- Plain-language explanation of the condition or topic
- How common it is in this species/breed/age group
- Brief explanation of the underlying cause or mechanism (simplified, no jargon)

**Signs and Symptoms**
- Common signs owners may notice at home
- How the condition typically progresses if untreated
- Why early detection matters

**Diagnosis**
- How veterinarians diagnose this condition
- Tests that may be recommended (explained simply)
- Why certain diagnostics are important

**Treatment Options**
- Overview of available treatment approaches
- What to expect during treatment
- Medications or procedures commonly used (explained in plain language)
- How quickly improvement is typically seen

**Home Management**
- Day-to-day care at home
- Dietary recommendations
- Exercise and activity guidance
- Monitoring tips for owners
- How to administer medications if needed

**Prognosis and Long-Term Outlook**
- Realistic expectations with proper treatment
- Factors that affect outcomes
- Quality of life considerations

**When to Call Your Veterinarian**
- Specific warning signs requiring prompt attention
- Signs of an emergency (go to emergency clinic immediately)
- Routine monitoring schedule recommendations

**Frequently Asked Questions**
- 3–5 common questions owners typically ask about this condition

**Support and Resources**
- Encouragement and reassurance for the owner
- Reminder that the veterinary team is there to help

Write in warm, accessible language at approximately an 8th-grade reading level. Use short paragraphs, bullet points, and clear headings. Avoid medical jargon; when technical terms are necessary, explain them in parentheses. The tone should be informative, compassionate, and empowering — helping owners feel confident in caring for their pet.
